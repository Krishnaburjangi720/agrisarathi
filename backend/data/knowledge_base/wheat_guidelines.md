# Wheat Farming Guidelines

**Source Reference:** Adapted from general FAO (Food and Agriculture Organization) and regional extension guidelines for wheat cultivation.

## Soil Requirements
Wheat grows best in well-drained loamy to clay-loam soils. The optimal soil pH is between 6.0 and 7.5. Good soil structure is important for root development. Saline or highly acidic soils severely restrict wheat yields.

## Irrigation
Wheat requires adequate water during key critical stages: crown root initiation (CRI), tillering, jointing, flowering, and grain filling. Rainfed wheat is possible depending on regional moisture, but supplementary irrigation at the CRI and flowering stages significantly improves yield.

## Risks
Environmental risks include early heat stress during the grain filling stage, which can lead to shriveled grains. Lodging due to heavy winds and over-fertilization is also a major risk. Prolonged waterlogging will stunt growth and should be prevented with good drainage.
